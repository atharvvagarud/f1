5. Ablation Study and Experimental Findings
5.1 Experimental Setup

All models were evaluated using GroupKFold cross-validation (5 folds), where each fold contained entire races (group = year_event_name). This ensured that no race appeared in both training and testing partitions, preventing leakage across driver instances within the same event.

Metrics are reported at the race level, not row level, and include:

Top-1 Accuracy – proportion of races where the true winner was ranked first.

Top-3 Accuracy – proportion of races where the true winner appeared within the top three predicted drivers.

Log Loss – probability calibration metric (lower is better).

All reported values are shown as mean ± standard deviation across folds.

5.2 Baseline Performance (Lap 10 Snapshot)

The strongest heuristic baseline is:

Leader at Lap 10 (position_x)

This baseline achieves strong Top-1 performance, demonstrating that race position at lap 10 already contains substantial predictive signal.

However:

Baselines do not produce probabilities.

They provide no calibration measure.

They cannot model interactions (e.g., tyre + weather + gap structure).

Thus, machine learning models are evaluated relative to this strong positional heuristic.

5.3 Non-Weather Models

Two supervised models were evaluated using strictly lap-10 information:

Logistic Regression

HistGradientBoostingClassifier (HGB)

Findings:

Logistic Regression provides a stable linear baseline.

HGB significantly improves log loss, indicating better probability calibration.

Top-1 accuracy remains competitive with the positional baseline but varies across folds.

This demonstrates that even without temporal modelling, tree-based methods can extract non-linear structure from early race-state features.

5.4 Weather Ablation

To evaluate whether environmental conditions improve predictive performance, weather variables were added:

Air temperature

Track temperature

Humidity

Pressure

Wind speed and direction

Rainfall

Binary rain indicator

This produced a second experimental track: Lap-10 + Weather.

Observations:

Weather-aware Logistic Regression shows marginal changes in Top-1.

Default HGB with weather improves probability calibration.

Tuned HGB (weather) produces the strongest calibrated performance overall.

However:

Top-1 accuracy of the tuned weather HGB is comparable to the strong positional baseline.

Improvements are more pronounced in log loss than in Top-1.

This suggests that:

At lap 10, positional information dominates winner prediction.

Weather contributes secondary contextual refinement.

Weather alone does not drastically alter early-race winner ranking.

5.5 Key Insight

The ablation demonstrates that:

Early-race position is the strongest single predictor.

Non-linear models improve probability quality.

Weather features provide moderate contextual gains.

There appears to be a performance ceiling for non-sequential models at lap 10.

This motivates the next stage of the project:

Investigating whether modelling temporal evolution across laps (e.g., LSTM or sequential models) provides additional predictive lift beyond static snapshot features.
